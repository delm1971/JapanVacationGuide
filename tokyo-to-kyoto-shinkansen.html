Train Types: Nozomi vs. Hikari vs. Kodama

Three distinct categories of bullet trains operate along the Tokaido Shinkansen line connecting Tokyo Station to Kyoto Station. All of them utilize the exact same physical train models, but they differ significantly in their speed, pricing, and number of stops:

  • Nozomi (The Fastest - ~2h 15m): The premium flagship express service. It stops only at major hubs like Yokohama and Nagoya. This train runs multiple times every single hour, making it the top choice for most vacationers.
  • Hikari (The Semi-Express - ~2h 40m): A slightly slower alternative that makes a handful of extra stops along the route. It takes roughly 25 minutes longer than the Nozomi.
  • Kodama (The Local - ~3h 40m): The slowest option. It stops at every single station along the entire path. It is rarely recommended for direct travel to Kyoto unless you buy a specialty discounted ticket.

Seat Classes and Booking Types

When purchasing your Tokaido Shinkansen tickets, you will need to select both your comfort class and your reservation preference:

1. Ordinary Car vs. Green Car (First Class)

The standard Ordinary Car features a clean, highly comfortable 3x2 seating row layout with generous legroom that easily beats standard western airline economy seating. Upgrading to a Green Car switches the configuration to a more spacious 2x2 layout, providing wider plush seats, built-in footrests, quieter cabin atmospheres, and hot towel hospitality services.

2. Reserved vs. Non-Reserved Seats

  • Reserved Seats (Recommended): Assigns you an exact car number and seat layout. This guarantees you can sit next to your travel companions and saves you from rushing onto the platform to secure a spot.
  • Non-Reserved Seats: Grants access to cars 1 through 3 on a first-come, first-served basis. While slightly cheaper, you run a high risk of standing in the aisle if traveling during morning commutes or busy tourist seasons.

The Mount Fuji Booking Trick: If you are traveling from Tokyo down to Kyoto, you can see Mount Fuji on the right side of the train roughly 40–45 minutes into the trip. To secure a window seat on the mountain side, make sure to explicitly request or select **Seat E** in an Ordinary Car row (or **Seat D** in a Green Car row)!

Onboard Amenities & Luggage Rules

Shinkansen travel is built around traveler convenience, but you must keep two critical guidelines in mind before boarding your train:

Feature / Rule What to Expect Important Travel Pro-Tip
Bento & Food Policy Eating and drinking are fully permitted. Every seat features a sturdy fold-down tray table. Buy an Ekiben (station lunch box) at Tokyo Station before boarding; it's a classic culinary tradition!
Oversized Luggage Rule Bags with total dimensions between 160cm and 250cm **require a mandatory special seat reservation**. Select a seat in the "Oversized Baggage Area" (the last row of the car) when purchasing tickets to avoid a ¥1,000 fine.
Power & Wi-Fi Free public Wi-Fi is available, and electrical outlets are installed at almost every seat row. Newer N700S train sets feature individual power outlets located directly on the armrest of every single seat.

How and When to Buy Tickets

Bullet train tickets officially release into the reservation system exactly **30 days prior** to your intended departure date. While you can usually find open seats on standard regular weekdays by walking up to a station machine, pre-booking online is absolutely mandatory if your itinerary falls during major national holiday windows:

  1. Cherry Blossom Season (Late March to Early April)
  2. Golden Week (Late April to Early May)
  3. Obon Festival (Mid-August)
  4. New Year's Holiday (Late December to Early January)
